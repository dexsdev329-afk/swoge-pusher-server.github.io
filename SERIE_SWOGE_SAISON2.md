# SWOGE SODA — SEASON 2 BIBLE

Vertical 9:16. Episodes ≈ 60s, cut from clips of MAX 8 seconds. ONE speaker per clip. All dialogue English, short. No on-screen text/subtitles. Every episode: 2s hook, varied cameras, varied settings, a fork, a vote-to-camera, a flicker beat. Characters LOCKED by reference image — treat the refs as law; this bible never redesigns them, only directs them.

## 1. SEASON 2 LOGLINE & ESCALATION

**Logline.** Fixing Leo's timeline left a tear in the apartment wall that leaks other realities — so now Leo's one-bedroom Paris flat is the crash site for the entire meme multiverse, and SWOGE has to keep a houseful of chaotic strangers alive while the tear tries to pull them all back out. Every week the apartment gets more crowded, the flicker-clock gets louder, and Leo has to decide, with the audience voting, who he actually trusts.

**How S2 escalates from S1 (bigger world, same heart).**
- **S1 was a duet; S2 is an ensemble.** Same apartment hub, same fridge, but now the door, the window, the fridge, and the drain are all unstable entry points. The flat stays the emotional center — everything comes home to Leo.
- **The stakes meter went external.** In S1 only SWOGE flickered (his existence was the clock). In S2 the whole apartment flickers when the tear is hungry — the stakes became a shared weather system, not one dog's problem.
- **The vote grew teeth.** S1 votes picked a timeline. S2 votes pick *who Leo listens to* — which means the audience is casting the ensemble's relationships in real time.
- **Same heart:** underneath the crowd, it's still a lonely broke guy and the dog who crossed time because he loves him. Every newcomer is a different mirror on that same ache — money, fame, love, obsolescence, belonging.

## 2. THE ENSEMBLE

Core retained: **SWOGE** (buff shiba from 2099, flickers electric-blue, deep rugged voice, dog paws never hands, black gym shorts or open red suit), **LEO** (broke deadpan 25-y/o, dry voice), **BRENDA** (passive-aggressive smart fridge). Their S1 rules are unchanged.

Each newcomer below is our own homage, NOT a branded copy.

### PEPE — the chill green frog from a parallel internet
- **Personality.** Serene on the surface, feral underneath. Total calm while describing insane things. Treats catastrophe as a vibe. The one who says the quiet, horrifying truth and then smiles.
- **Voice.** Slow, soft, unbothered. Mid register, long pauses — the opposite of SWOGE's bark. (Keep each clip one-speaker; Pepe's silence does a lot of the acting.)
- **Running gag.** Pepe has already accepted every outcome, including the bad ones. He narrates disasters as if reading a spa menu. He keeps trying to "feel" human appliances (sits on the radiator, meditates on the microwave).
- **Catchphrase.** *"It's fine. It's all fine."* (deadpan, while it is visibly not fine)
- **Flicker / arrival.** Pepe seeped in through the drain; he arrives damp and calm. His flicker is **green and slow — a gentle pulse**, the opposite of SWOGE's panic-blue strobe. When Pepe is lying, the green goes still (too calm).
- **Theme he unlocks:** loneliness, mental health, internet nihilism, finding peace vs. faking it. Pepe is the show's "it's okay to not be okay" engine — played straight-faced.

### DOGE — the wise, old-meme shiba who speaks in fragments
- **Personality.** Ancient, gentle, a little lost in time. He was a legend before SWOGE existed; now he's the elder nobody quotes anymore. Kind. Forgets what year it is. Occasionally drops a line of devastating wisdom by accident.
- **Voice.** Frail, warm, unhurried. Speaks in broken fragments — adjective, noun, pause. (Keep clips short; his fragments ARE short dialogue, perfect for the pipeline.)
- **Running gag.** He talks in the old broken-caption cadence — "much tired. very apartment. so tear." — and everyone has to translate. He mistakes new tech for magic and magic for Tuesday.
- **Catchphrase.** *"Much... everything."* (said looking at the whole crowded room)
- **Flicker / arrival.** Doge faded in slowly, like an old photo developing, through the window. His flicker is **amber and flickery like a dying bulb** — he's the character closest to being pulled back, so his light is the weakest. Visual shorthand: when Doge's amber steadies, he remembered something that matters.
- **Theme he unlocks:** aging, obsolescence, legacy, being forgotten, what we owe the people who came before. Doge is the heart-punch character. SWOGE is terrified he'll become Doge.

### SHIBA — the fierce rival shiba, SWOGE's cocky counterpart
- **Personality.** Everything SWOGE is, turned up and turned mean. Cocky, competitive, allergic to sincerity. Secretly insecure. Wants Leo to pick HIM as the "real" dog. The antagonist you kind of root for.
- **Voice.** Sharp, fast, cocky — higher and snappier than SWOGE's deep rumble, so viewers never confuse the two shibas by audio alone (important: two similar refs, keep voices far apart).
- **Running gag.** Turns everything into a contest SWOGE didn't agree to enter. Flexes at inanimate objects. Keeps "winning" things that don't matter and losing the one that does (Leo).
- **Catchphrase.** *"Second best. Must hurt."* (to SWOGE, grinning)
- **Flicker / arrival.** Shiba kicked the door in; he arrives mid-flex. His flicker is **red-blue clashing with SWOGE's** — when they're in frame their flickers fight, strobing out of sync. Visual stakes: when their flickers SYNC, they're finally on the same side (big moment, used sparingly).
- **Theme he unlocks:** ego, rivalry, masculinity played for laughs, jealousy, fame/clout, the fear of being replaced. Shiba is the clout-chaser; he thinks the tear is a stage.

### ANDY & BRETT — the two chaotic goofy buddies who arrive together
- **Personality.** A double act. ANDY is the overconfident idea-man who's always wrong with full conviction; BRETT is the loyal hype-man who commits 100% to Andy's worst plans. They cause 80% of the season's property damage. They are best friends and that's the joke and the heart.
- **Voices.** Andy = loud, up-talking, certain. Brett = even louder, agreeing. **Never in the same clip** — our pipeline is one-speaker-per-clip, so Andy & Brett are built as rapid A/B shot-reverse-shot. Their comedy IS the cut.
- **Running gag.** "Plan." Andy announces a Plan; Brett repeats the Plan louder; the Plan immediately destroys something; they high-five anyway. Recurring bit: they keep "fixing" the tear and keep making it bigger.
- **Catchphrases.** Andy: *"Trust the plan."* — Brett: *"I trust the plan."* (said right before disaster)
- **Flicker / arrival.** They tumbled out of the FRIDGE together (Brenda is furious). Their flicker is **a shared orange one that strobes in rhythm with each other**, never alone — if you see one buddy's flicker without the other's, something has gone wrong (used for one gut-punch late in the season).
- **Theme they unlock:** friendship, chaos, bystander internet culture, "we have no idea what we're doing and we're doing it together," found family. Also the show's pure-fun pressure valve between heavier episodes.

### BRENDA (returning) — the smart fridge
- Now effectively the apartment's **landlord of the multiverse**: she logs every arrival, resents every one, and has opinions. She's the one appliance old enough to remember S1, so she's Leo's dry mirror and the show's running commentary. New S2 gag: she rates each newcomer's "tenancy." Catchphrase stays passive-aggressive: *"Noted. Again."*

## 3. FLICKER + VOTE MECHANICS (evolved for an ensemble)

**The flicker becomes a color-coded ensemble language.** Each character has their own flicker color/rhythm (blue-panic SWOGE, green-calm Pepe, amber-fading Doge, red-cocky Shiba, orange-shared Andy/Brett). This does three jobs at once:
1. **Instant readability** on mute, mid-scroll (no subtitles allowed — color carries meaning).
2. **A stakes meter per character** — whoever's flicker is strobing hardest is closest to being yanked back through the tear this episode.
3. **Relationship signaling** — flickers that SYNC = alliance; flickers that CLASH = conflict. The apartment-wide flicker (everything in frame pulsing) = the tear is "hungry," the episode's clock.

**The vote evolves from timeline-picking to trust-casting.**
- S1: "A or B" chose which timeline we open on.
- S2: the fork is now usually **"Who does Leo trust? A: [character] / B: [character]"** or **"Who does Leo save first? A / B."** The audience is literally assembling the ensemble's loyalties and the pecking order.
- **Winning vote opens next episode** on that timeline, exactly like S1 — so the cast you built is canon.
- **Escalation device:** a few mid-season votes are **"keep them or send them back"** — the tear offers to close if Leo gives one traveler up. That turns the audience into the one deciding who gets to belong. Maximum participation, maximum guilt, maximum comments.
- The 4th-wall ask stays SWOGE's job as host (deep voice, straight to camera) — but late season, other characters start fighting for the camera to make their own case to voters, which becomes a plot point (Shiba tries to campaign; Pepe tells the truth; Doge forgets to ask).

## 4. SEASON ARC + PER-EPISODE THEMES

**The overarching threat/mystery: THE TEAR.** The hole SWOGE made saving Leo isn't just leaking travelers in — it's a pull that wants its people *back*, and it's getting stronger each time the apartment gets more crowded. The mystery: **why these five, why this flat, why now?** The slow reveal: the tear isn't random. It's pulling in the exact people Leo needs to not become the lonely, closed-off man who, in the original future, made the choice that erased SWOGE. The tear is, in a sense, SWOGE's love for Leo running in reverse — trying to assemble a family around him before the clock runs out. The season villain isn't a person; it's **the version of Leo who stops letting people in.**

**8–10 episode map (each a self-contained theme, each feeding the arc):**

1. **"Full House"** — *theme: belonging / overwhelm.* Pepe seeps up the drain; the tear is now a crowd problem. Hook: the flat's wall is breathing. Fork vote: **A: let the strangers stay / B: seal the wall.** Establishes ensemble + color-flicker language.

2. **"So Tired"** — *theme: aging & obsolescence.* Doge arrives, amber and fading. SWOGE sees his own future in Doge and panics. Fork: **A: SWOGE helps Doge remember / B: SWOGE avoids him.** Heart episode.

3. **"Second Best"** — *theme: ego, rivalry, clout.* Shiba kicks the door in; two shibas, one Leo. Clashing flickers. Fork vote reframed as trust: **A: Leo trusts SWOGE / B: Leo gives Shiba a chance.** First "trust-cast" vote.

4. **"Trust the Plan"** — *theme: friendship & chaos.* Andy & Brett fall out of Brenda. Their "fix" doubles the tear's size. Pure comedy breather with a time-bomb ending. Fork: **A: follow Andy's plan / B: do literally nothing.**

5. **"It's Fine"** — *theme: loneliness / mental health, played straight.* Quiet bottle-ish episode. Pepe and Leo on the floor at 3am. The flat goes still-green. The emotional core of the season stated out loud. Fork: **A: Leo admits he's lonely / B: Leo says he's fine.**

6. **"Send One Back"** — *theme: who belongs / sacrifice.* MIDSEASON TURN. The tear offers to close if Leo surrenders one traveler. Everyone's flicker spikes. The audience holds the knife. Fork: **A: give up [X] / B: refuse and keep the clock running.** Highest-stakes vote of the season.

7. **"Campaign"** — *theme: fame & manipulation / internet culture.* Shiba figures out the 4th wall and starts campaigning to voters directly, turning the comments into a popularity war. Meta episode about clout and audiences. Fork: **A: vote honest / B: vote for the loudest.** (The show lightly roasts its own mechanic.)

8. **"The Old Legend"** — *theme: legacy & memory.* Doge's amber nearly dies; the cast learns WHO he was and why the tear wants its elder. Doge's one steady-amber moment delivers the mystery's key clue. Fork: **A: save Doge / B: save the clue.**

9. **"The Hungry Wall"** — *theme: all-in, found family.* The apartment-wide flicker never stops now. The tear reveals it's mirroring Leo's own closing-off. The ensemble has to choose to stay *for* Leo even though staying feeds the pull. Fork: **A: everyone holds the line together / B: everyone runs.**

10. **"The Choice, Again" (finale)** — *theme: love vs. fear, the whole season's thesis.* Mirrors the S1 finale's shape: Leo faces the same kind of choice that once erased SWOGE, but now with a houseful of people he let in. Flickers get the chance to all SYNC for the first time. Ends on the biggest vote yet — **the fate of the apartment and everyone in it — A / B — into S3.**

*(Flexible to 8: fold "Campaign" into "Send One Back," and merge 9–10, if a tighter run is needed.)*

## 5. RUNNING MOTIFS + QUOTABLE CATCHPHRASES

**Season-wide motifs:**
- **The breathing wall / the tear** — a recurring visual beat; the apartment inhales before every arrival. (Reliable 2s hook generator.)
- **Color-flicker language** — blue/green/amber/red/orange; sync = love, clash = conflict, all-pulse = clock. The show's signature, readable on mute.
- **Brenda's tenancy log** — a recurring dry button on each newcomer.
- **"Future-logic, useless"** — SWOGE's S1 comedy engine continues: he keeps offering 2099 solutions that don't work in 2026.
- **The couch** — the whole ensemble slowly, physically, crowds onto Leo's one small couch. The couch filling up IS the season's emotional arithmetic.
- **The one-speaker rule turned into style** — reaction cuts, shot-reverse-shot (esp. Andy/Brett), and silent flicker beats are the house grammar, not a limitation.

**Quotable lines (one signature per character, built to be commented/duetted):**

- **LEO:** *"Cool. Normal apartment stuff."* (deadpan, to any catastrophe)
- **BRENDA:** *"Noted. Again."*
- **PEPE:** *"It's fine. It's all fine."*
- **DOGE:** *"Much... everything."*
- **SHIBA:** *"Second best. Must hurt."*
- **ANDY:** *"Trust the plan."* / **BRETT:** *"I trust the plan."*

## 6. WHY THIS DRIVES VIRALITY (across all possible themes)

- **Serialized drama + a cliffhanger engine.** The winning vote literally opens the next episode, so watching without voting means losing input over canon. FOMO is built into the format.
- **Participation is now casting.** Trust-casting and "send one back" votes make the audience responsible for relationships and sacrifices — that's argument fuel in the comments, which is the single biggest driver of reach. People don't share a video; they share a side.
- **Recognizable meme ensemble, legally clean.** Each newcomer is an homage archetype (chill frog, old shiba, rival shiba, two chaos buddies) — instantly legible to internet-native viewers, zero brand risk, infinite reaction-meme potential.
- **Absurd played straight.** The comedy comes from treating a multiverse housing crisis like a lease dispute; the drama comes from meaning every emotional beat. Played-straight absurdity is the most duet-able, stitch-able tone there is.
- **"All themes" via the ensemble.** Each character is a theme-key, so any trend or mood has a native door in: loneliness (Pepe), aging/legacy (Doge), ego/fame (Shiba), friendship/chaos (Andy & Brett), love/fear (SWOGE+Leo), AI/consumer tech (Brenda). The show can chase any cultural moment without breaking canon.
- **Mute-first design.** Color-flicker language means the emotional state reads instantly on a muted scroll — then the one-speaker short dialogue rewards the unmute. Built for the feed, top to bottom.
- **The heart keeps it rewatchable.** Chaos gets the click; the lonely-guy-and-his-dog core gets the save, the follow, and the return next week.
